01

The mobile menu button did nothing

Fixed 2.1.1 Keyboard · A 2.4.5 Multiple Ways · AA

Below 900 px the header hides both the primary navigation and the search field, and the burger button that replaced them had no behaviour attached — no handler, no panel, no drawer element anywhere in the document. Phone users had no route into the site except the footer.

Built a real drawer in assets/js/a11y.js: it carries the eleven category links, the search field and the three utility links. It is modal while open — focus moves into the panel, wraps at both ends, the page behind is locked, and Esc, the close button, the scrim, or following a link all dismiss it and return focus to the burger.

Serious — Level A / AA failures

03

The page scrolled sideways at 320 px

Fixed1.4.10 Reflow · AA

Two separate causes, and the second only became visible once the first was cleared.

  • Below 560 px the promo cards combined aspect-ratio:1/1 with min-height:340px. The square ratio then forced the width to 340 px too, inside a 320 px viewport. Dropping the ratio at that breakpoint kept the height.
  • The three ESG stat columns used repeat(3,1fr), whose implicit auto minimum held the grid 9 px wider than the screen. Changed to repeat(3,minmax(0,1fr)), and they now stack to two columns below 560 px.

My original report named .proof__l as an overflow source. That was a false lead — an ancestor clips it, so it never extended the document. The real causes are the two above.

05

The brand marquee scrolled forever with no way to stop it

Fixed2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide · A

A 38-second animation on infinite repeat beside static content. The build already honoured prefers-reduced-motion, which helps a subset of users, but the criterion asks for a control on the page.

Added a labelled toggle above the rail. It flips aria-pressed, swaps its own text between “Pause logo scroll” and “Play logo scroll”, and drives animation-play-state. Where the OS already requests reduced motion the track is not animating, so the control removes itself rather than sitting there inert.

06

Two text colours fell under the contrast threshold

Fixed1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum) · AA

Brand red #E50E46 holds on white but not on the grey band. Added --red-text:#C40A39 as the text tint and pointed .eyebrow at it, leaving --red for fills, buttons and icons where contrast rules do not apply.

The T-shirt card took three attempts and is worth recording, because the first two made it worse. Its photo runs from near-white highlights to deep shadow directly under the copy:

  • Deepening the dark scrim dropped the ink heading from 2.99:1 to 2.57:1 — a darker ground lowers contrast for dark text, not raises it.
  • Flipping the copy to white on that darker scrim was worse again at 2.04:1, because white text then failed on the photo’s bright highlights.
  • Lightening the ground instead works: a white wash under the ink copy, which also keeps this card consistent with its two siblings rather than making it the odd one out.

The newsletter field collects information about the user, so it must expose its purpose programmatically. Added autocomplete="email" and name="email"; the two search fields got name="q" with autocomplete="off", which is correct for a site search rather than a personal-data field.

Fixed2.4.7 Focus Visible · AA

outline:none with only a 1 px border-colour change as replacement — a technical pass, but far weaker than the 2 px crimson rings the buttons and tabs use. Removed both outline:none declarations and added matching :focus-visible rings, so the whole page is now consistent.

A generic div has no role, and ARIA prohibits naming it, so the social icon group was announced as four unrelated links. Changed to <nav aria-label="Social media">. This was the sole aria-prohibited-attr item in the scan; it is now gone.

Withdrawn after re-testing

12

“Card copy is clipped under text-spacing overrides”

Withdrawn1.4.12 Text Spacing · AA

My original evidence was a scrollHeight of 366 px against a clientHeight of 340 px on the promo cards, which I read as the CTA pill being cut off. Re-testing element by element shows that delta is the decorative ::before blur layer, which is deliberately inset by −18 px on all sides and clipped by design.

With the criterion’s mandated overrides applied, zero text-bearing elements overflow their clipping container, and both the copy block and the CTA measure fully inside the card. 1.4.12 was already passing. I had over-read a scroll-metric without confirming which box actually overflowed.

The layout change was kept regardless — the copy and CTA now sit in normal flow instead of being absolutely positioned, so the card grows with its content rather than relying on the square having spare room. That is a robustness improvement, not a defect fix.

Open — needs a decision, not a fix

13

Placeholders are still doing the work of labels

Open3.3.2 Labels or Instructions · A

Both fields carry a correct aria-label, so the accessible name is present and this is not a failure. But no label is visible, and the placeholder disappears the moment someone types — which disproportionately affects users with cognitive or memory difficulties. A persistent visible label above the newsletter field is the safer pattern. Left alone because it changes the visual design, which is your call rather than mine.

On minimum font size

Asked during the fix pass, and worth writing down because the premise is a common misconception.

WCAG sets no minimum font size. There is no success criterion at any level that names a pixel or point floor. What the guidelines require instead is that text survives being made bigger and re-spaced: 1.4.4 Resize Text (200% without loss of content or function), 1.4.12 Text Spacing, and 1.4.10 Reflow. This page passes all three.

Size does change one thing: the contrast bar. Below 18.66 px bold or 24 px regular, text is “small” and needs 4.5:1 rather than 3:1. Almost everything on this page is small text by that definition, which is why the eyebrow fix mattered.

As a practical recommendation rather than a conformance one, I would lift the 9.5 px .prod__flag badges to 11—12 px. They are uppercase, letter-spaced, and sit on photography, which is the hardest combination to read at that size, and they carry real information (“Next day”) rather than decoration.

One thing to leave alone: three elements compute to font-size:0 below 560 px. That is the deliberate icon-only treatment for the Account, Basket and Menu controls. The text is still in the DOM and still forms the accessible name, so screen readers announce them normally.

Method and remaining scope

How contrast over images was measured. Each photo was drawn to a canvas at its painted size with the same object-fit: cover geometry, the scrim gradient composited at the exact position of every text run, and the WCAG relative-luminance ratio computed against the worst pixel under the glyphs. This is what resolved all 77 of axe’s undetermined contrast nodes, and what caught the T-shirt card failing by 0.01.

Files changed. index.html, assets/css/style.css, and a new assets/js/a11y.js. The stylesheet cache-buster moved to ?v=8.

Still not covered. Screen-reader narration has not been verified with NVDA, JAWS or VoiceOver — recommended before sign-off, particularly for the new drawer and the tab components. Motion sensitivity (2.3.1), colour-blindness simulation and the AAA criteria remain out of scope. Only index.html was audited; findings 01, 02, 04, 08 and 09 live in shared header, footer and CSS, so the same fixes will carry to every other page built on them — but each page needs its own reflow and contrast check.
